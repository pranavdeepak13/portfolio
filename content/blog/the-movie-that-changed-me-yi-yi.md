Yi Yi ( A One and A Two ) is one of the films that doesn't hit you after the first watch but stays with you once you go through certain phases in life. As people say, once we evolve after facing a variety of experiences and when life becomes monotonous, I would say that would be a perfect time for a rewatch of Yi Yi. That's when I found the real comfort in experiencing what Yi Yi had to offer when I had watched it the first time.
Some films announce what they are about. *Yi Yi* does not. It's not ostentatious with respect to  to cinematography, editing or any other technical aspects to make the movie a visual spectacle but yet it is one of the most beautiful films I have ever watched.
Its about a family in Taipei spanning over a year and lets the weight of ordinary days gather slowly: a wedding, career issues, marriage struggles, first love, a grandmother in comatose, children trying to make sense of the adults around them.

Edward Yang's 2000 film begins with a wedding and ends with a funeral that's what makes the film feel like it completes a cycle. In between, it follows the Jian family without forcing any one of them to carry the whole film. [Criterion's synopsis](https://www.criterion.com/films/781-yi-yi) describes that shape plainly, but the experience of watching it is harder to reduce. The film keeps moving its attention. A small hurt in one room changes the atmosphere in another.

This scene where she breaks down and the stoic NJ handling her is one of pure emotional expression which will make you tear up if you're going through the similar experiences in life. I was moved by the acting and staging of the entire scene which made it one of my favourites.

## A family is never only one story

When I first saw  *Yi Yi* I thought it was a melodramatic family drama. I left thinking about how much of a life is lived beside other people, not directly with them. NJ, Ting-Ting, Min-Min, and Yang-Yang are close enough to affect one another every day, yet each person carries something the others cannot fully see. That's the part which resonates with me the most, we people can't see what other people are going through completely because we can't even see certain parts of ourselves too. The film gives its youngest character, Yang-Yang, a camera. He photographs the backs of people's heads because they cannot see that part of themselves. It is a childlike gesture, but it gives the film one of its clearest ideas: another person can show you a piece of your life that was always there, just outside your view.
[Yang-Yang holding his camera at the doorway.](/images/blog/yi-yi-yang-yang-camera.png) 
When I watched the film the second time I had viewed it through the lens of Yang-Yang and how he tries to make sense of all the adults and their actions. Eventhough people share a home and still miss the point of each other. 
They make decisions from old grief, private embarrassment, or an image of the future that nobody else has been shown.
NJ is the character most people would resonate with when going through mid-life crisis and all the things he does, I personally connected with the most.
Min-Min and her relationship with her grandmother is something I miss in my real life and the way she navigates guilt, love and loss is scripted beautifully which cannot be explained but can only be felt.
As Mr Ota says " You don't live the same day twice", though they might seem monotonous,ordinary it's how we percieve the day to make it matter and they never are the same. 
An explicit shoutout to the static cinematography which is a pillar of strength of the movies narrative style and is a testament to the statement 
"The heart and mind are the true lens of the camera".

Enjoy the ordinary life, Celebrate the simplicity.
## The part I want to keep

I still have my own memory to put beside this film. It belongs with the scenes that made me stop treating daily life as something which is regular : the quiet rooms, the conversations that arrive late, and the people who are trying their best without knowing what they are missing.


*Yi Yi* changed me because it made that attention feel like enough. Nothing needs to explode for a day to matter.
